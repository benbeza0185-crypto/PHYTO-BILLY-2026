Plant diseases and protection

Learn common symptoms, prevention and responsible treatment.

Fungal disease

Early blight

Can affect tomato and potato leaves. Look for brown spots that may have ring patterns.

Prevention: Crop rotation, healthy planting materials and good airflow.

Fungal disease

Late blight

A serious disease of potato and tomato. It can spread quickly in cool, wet weather.

Prevention: Monitor crops, use healthy planting materials and seek early advice.

Bacterial disease

Bacterial wilt

May cause sudden wilting even when soil is moist.

Prevention: Clean planting material, crop rotation and good field hygiene.

Fungal disease

Anthracnose

Can affect beans and other crops, causing dark lesions on leaves, stems or pods.

Prevention: Healthy seed, crop rotation and removal of infected crop remains.

Leaf disease

Leaf spot

Different fungi and bacteria can cause spots on leaves. The appearance depends on the cause.

Prevention: Avoid overcrowding and keep tools and planting materials clean.

Root problem

Root rot

Often associated with waterlogged soil and harmful soil organisms.

Prevention: Improve drainage and avoid overwatering.

A symptom can have several causes. Do not choose a pesticide based only on a website description. Confirm the problem with an agricultural officer and use only locally approved products according to their labels.

Plant nutrition centre

Understand the nutrients plants need to grow.

Nitrogen (N)

Supports leaf growth and the formation of proteins. A shortage may cause older leaves to become pale or yellow.

Phosphorus (P)

Supports root development, energy transfer and flowering. Availability depends partly on soil conditions.

Potassium (K)

Supports water regulation, plant strength and many plant processes.

Calcium

Helps build strong cell walls and supports healthy growing tissues.

Magnesium

An important part of chlorophyll, which helps plants capture light energy.

Compost and soil

Compost can improve soil structure and add organic matter. Use mature compost and follow local soil advice.
